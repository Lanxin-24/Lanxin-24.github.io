About Me

I am a master's student in Biostatistics at the University of Washington. Before joining UW, I received my B.S. in Information and Computing Science from the School of Mathematical Sciences at Fudan University.

My research interests lie at the intersection of mathematical statistics, statistical learning, and computational genomics, with a particular interest in statistical genetics, high-dimensional inference, and single-cell transcriptomics.

Research

Single-cell Allele-specific Expression (ASE)

University of Washington

I am investigating statistical and computational approaches to allele-specific expression analysis in single-cell RNA sequencing, with a focus on statistical genetics and method development.

Gene Selection and Phenotype Reconstruction Based on Pre-Ranking and Differentiable Sampling

Fudan University

Developed a two-stage framework integrating rank-based convex optimization and differentiable sampling for marker gene selection in high-dimensional single-cell RNA-seq data. The framework reduced the initial gene space by 87% and improved cell-type classification and phenotype reconstruction accuracy by 4.1% and 5.8%, respectively, across test datasets.

Cell Classification and Spatial Visualization Using RNA-seq Data

Fudan University

Studied dimensionality reduction and unsupervised clustering of single-cell RNA-seq data using PCA, UMAP, K-means, and model-based clustering in R. Parameter tuning improved the silhouette coefficient from 0.53 to 0.85.
